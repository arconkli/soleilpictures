The board is for arranging. Files is for finding.

Same cluster, same contents, different question being asked — and you can ask
both at once.

## Opening the full Files page

A cluster's files are always listed in the sidebar's
[Files card](/docs/clusters/sidebar#files). When you want only files, press
`F`, or ⤢ at the top right of that card, and they fill the screen. Each
shelf's ⤢ in the sidebar opens the page on just that kind of file.

The page is that card grown: the cluster's name at the top, the same search
and **All files ⌄** menu, and the same shelves. While it's open, the card in
the sidebar folds to a bar — **Files · full screen** — so your files are
listed once.

| To do this | Do this |
|---|---|
| Open the full Files page | `F`, or ⤢ on the sidebar's Files card |
| Go back to the board | `F` again, `⌘\`, or **Shrink** — at the top right of the page, or on the folded Files card in the sidebar |
| Find a file on the board | **Show on board** in its menu, in Quick look or in Info — the board brings it into view and selects it |
| Put a file somewhere on the board | Drag it from the sidebar's Files onto the board |

`⌘K` → "Show Files" does the same as `F`. Opening the page this way is yours
alone, on this device; it never changes the cluster for anyone else. To make a
cluster open in Files for everyone, and on shared and published links, choose
**Always open in Files** from its menu in the sidebar (if you can edit it);
the same menu turns it back with **Open on the board by default**. Visitors
on those links get this same page, read-only: the search, **All files ⌄**,
Grid and List, Quick look, Info and downloads, but nothing that adds, moves
or deletes. The title is just the cluster's name there, and its clusters open
from their tiles.

In split view, `F` acts on the pane you last worked in, and Files there fills
that pane.

A folder or zip you [import](/docs/clusters#importing-folders-and-zips) puts its
files in each new cluster's Files, off the board, and the empty canvas offers to
put them on.

## Dragging files

Files dragged from this page onto a folder move into that cluster, as they do
from the sidebar. Hold `⌥` (`Ctrl` on Windows) as you let go to copy them
instead: a copy is a new card showing the same stored file, so nothing is
uploaded again and it takes no extra storage, but it counts toward your plan's
card limit. The rules, and what a move takes with it, are on the sidebar page
under [Moving and copying files](/docs/clusters/sidebar#moving-and-copying-files).

## Not on board

A file can be in a cluster without being on its board. Files you add in Files
start that way, and so does a card you take off: right-click it on the board
and choose **Remove from board**. Either way it waits in Files with a **Not on
board** mark. **Not on board** in the **View** menu shows just those.

To put one on the board, drag it there from the sidebar, or right-click it in Files — or use
the selection bar, or Quick look — and choose **Put on board**, which places it
in the clear space nearest the middle of where the board was last looking. A
link or search result that points at a file that isn't on the board opens Files
with it picked out instead.

Files waiting off the board are still cards: they count toward your plan's
card limit, and anyone the cluster is shared with sees them in Files.

## Folders and the path bar

A cluster's nested clusters are its folders. They come first — in the grid as
tiles along the top, with the cluster's colour dot (or its cover), its name
and how many items are inside, and in the list as the table's first rows —
with the files after them.

**Double-click a folder to open it.** You go there and stay in Files, and the
cluster above it sits over the title as **‹ its name** — click it to go up.
The full path is in the breadcrumb at the top of the screen.

### Springing into folders while you drag

As in Finder, you can move something into a folder you can't see yet. Drag a
file (or a folder), **hold it over a folder for a moment** and Files opens that
folder with the drag still in your hand; hold it over **Back** or a step of the
path to go back up. Let go on the folder's files, or on a folder inside it, to
move what you're dragging there. The folder blinks just before it opens.

You land in that cluster once the move is saved. Let go anywhere else, or press
`Esc`, and Files goes back to where the drag started.

## Grid and list

**Grid** — the sidebar card's shelves, grown. **Photos** and **Video** are a
gallery laid out in rows that fill the width: pictures keep their own shape — a
portrait stays tall, a panorama stays wide — instead of being cropped to a box,
and the picture is the name; hover one to read it. **Audio** and **Files**
follow as rows, and **Notes**, **Links** and **Other** start folded — click
one's name to open it. The default, because most of what you are looking for
you would recognise by sight. **Size** in the **All files ⌄** menu — Small,
Medium or Large — sets how big the pictures are, remembered on your device.

**List** — a classic list: one table of everything, clusters first, with
**Kind**, **Size** and the date, every other row faintly shaded. Dense,
scannable, sortable — click a column's heading. A cluster that is mostly audio
opens in the list, for the columns below.

**Grid** and **List** sit beside the search (on a phone or tablet, in the
**All files ⌄** menu). Your choice is remembered per cluster on the device you made it on.

## When a cluster is mostly audio

The list swaps its columns. **Kind** and **Size** give way to **Time**,
**BPM**, **Key** and **Format**, and each one sorts — so a folder of samples
becomes something you can order by tempo or by key.

It switches on its own once half of what you are looking at is audio, which is
also true the moment you filter to Audio. Nothing to turn on, and a cluster of
notes and images never grows four empty columns.

**Key sorts by the circle of fifths, not the alphabet.** Sorting a pack by key
is really asking what stacks with what, so C, G, D, A and E land next to each
other rather than A, B, C.

Tempo and key come from the filename as the file uploads, and are editable on
the card — see [video and audio](/docs/files/video-and-audio).

Beside the search — under it on a phone or tablet — you get the pack in one
line: **how many audio files, the tempo range they span, and how much material
there is in total**. It follows the search and the filters, so narrowing to
`128` re-reads the selection you are actually looking at.

On a narrow window, in a split pane, or with Info open, the columns that
matter least give way first, until on a phone only the name is left. The name
is the last thing to give up room.

## Sorting and filtering

Both live in the **All files ⌄** menu beside the search field — the same menu,
in the same words, as the sidebar's Files card.

Files opens **newest first**, by date added. The menu also sorts by **Name**,
**Size** or **Canvas order**: the canvas read like a page, top row first and
each row left to right, so what you see is the board you just left, lined up.
In the list, click a column's heading to sort by it — name, kind, size or date,
plus **time**, **tempo**, **key** and **format** when the cluster is mostly
audio — and the menu shows that sort as the one in force.

**Show** narrows to one shelf — Photos, Video, Audio, Files, Notes or Links,
whichever the cluster has — or to files that are **Not on board**, and the
menu takes its name: **Photos ⌄**.

**Search** filters as you type, against names and content.

The combination is the answer to "where is that PDF someone dropped in here last
week" — show the **Files** shelf, newest first, done. On the canvas that is a
hunting expedition.

## Selecting and moving

Click a file to select it, `⌘`-click to add to the selection, `⇧`-click for a
range, `⌘A` for everything showing. In the grid, the round check that appears
in a tile's corner on hover adds it without a modifier key. Click empty space
or press `Esc` to clear it. The arrow keys move a highlight through the files,
`Enter` selects it and `⇧Space` adds it to the selection. `Tab` reaches each
folder (`Enter` opens it) and then the files, which take one `Tab` stop between
them: the arrows move inside it.

Select something and the search line gives way to a bar:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Download** | The files, as a zip when there's more than one — see below |
| **Move to…** | Pick another cluster; the cards leave this one and arrive there, with undo |
| **Delete** | Removes them, with undo |

Dragging files onto one of the folders above them moves them into that
cluster too — and holding them over a folder opens it, so you can drop them
deeper (see [Springing into folders](#springing-into-folders-while-you-drag)).

### With a finger

On a touch screen a tap opens what it lands on, as the iPad's Files app does: a
folder opens and a file opens in [Quick look](#quick-look). Touch and hold a
file or a folder for its menu, the same one a right-click opens, with
**Select** first. **Select** puts a check on every file, and each tap then adds
one or takes it out; a hold adds the one it is on and opens the menu for them
all. The bar gains **Copy to…**, since there is no right-click
to find it, and ends with **Done**. A hold opens the menu rather than picking a
file up, so a finger moves files with **Move to…** or **Put on board**, not by
dragging. A trackpad or mouse on a tablet still clicks and selects as it does
on a computer.

**Right-click** a file, a folder or a selection for everything you can do
with it — Quick look, Show on board, Rename, Download, Copy link, Move to…,
Copy to…, Get info and Delete — or press `⇧F10` to open the same menu from the
keyboard.

**Rename** a file or folder with `F2`, or Rename in that menu: the name
becomes editable in place, `Enter` saves and `Esc` puts it back. Renaming a
file changes what it's called in the cluster, not the file — it still
downloads under the name it was uploaded with. Notes are named by their text,
so they don't rename. `⌘Z` and `⌘⇧Z` undo and redo in Files just as they do on the
board.

## Auditioning audio

An audio row's thumbnail is a play button. Press it and the clip plays in
place. The waveform beside it is drawn from the file itself, and it **fills as
the clip plays**. While it is playing, click anywhere along that waveform to
jump there — the fastest way to the part of a long sample you actually want. A
line fills along the bottom of the row as well, for when the waveform has no
room.

In the **grid**, audio has a shelf of its own, as rows with the same play
button and waveform.

For going through a lot of them, use the keyboard: `↑` and `↓` move a
highlight down the rows (the arrows walk the tiles in the grid), `Space` plays
whatever is highlighted, `Enter` selects it. **When
a clip finishes, the next audio row starts on its own** and the highlight
follows — so a pack of loops plays through while you keep your hands still. It
stops at the end rather than wrapping.

Only one thing plays at a time, everywhere: starting a row stops a clip playing
on the canvas, and vice versa. Sorting by tempo or key and then holding `↓` is
the fastest way through a folder of samples.

## Downloading

Hover any row holding a file — an image, PDF, video, audio clip or attachment —
and a download button appears at the end of it. Files come down under their
**original names**, so renaming a card for readability never costs you the
extension. Images and videos uploaded before they started keeping names come
down under the card's title, or a generic name if it has none. A name a browser invents for a pasted image — `image.png` — is
not kept.

**Download all** sits at the top right of the page, beside **Shrink**, whenever
there is more than one file to take (on a phone or tablet, select them and use
**Download** on the bar). It follows the search and the filters, so it reads *Download 14* once you
have narrowed to fourteen. Select particular rows instead and the selection bar
offers **Download** for just those.

Either way you get a single zip named after the cluster. Zips are capped at
**{{fact:zipMaxFiles}} files** or **{{fact:zipMaxSize}}**, whichever comes
first; past either, take it in batches.

Anything in the selection with no file behind it — a note, a link — is skipped,
and the count of what was skipped is reported rather than quietly dropped. The
same is true of a file storage cannot hand back: the archive still contains
everything that could be read, and says how many could not.

## Previews

Everything gets a real preview, not a generic icon. Images and video show
themselves; [grids](/docs/canvas/grids), [docs](/docs/documents),
shapes, notes and links get schematic marks
that indicate their shape and content.

## Quick look

Double-click a file, or highlight it and press `Space`, to see it big without
leaving Files. Pictures open fitted — click to see them at full size and drag
to look around — video and audio play, PDFs page through with `↑` / `↓`, and
so do [documents](/docs/files) (Word, PowerPoint, Excel, Keynote and the rest),
and everything else shows its preview large. `←` / `→` step to the previous and
next file, in the order Files is showing them. `Space` or `Esc` closes it.

A bar floats along the top: the file's name and where it is among the files
(*Image · 3 of 30*), then **Show on board** (or **Show in cluster**, for a file
you're browsing in another cluster), **Download** and **Info**.

On an audio row in the List layout, `Space` still auditions it in place, so a
pack plays through with `↓` and `Space`.

## Info

**Info** — `⌘I`, **Get info** in the right-click menu, or the button in Quick
look and on the selection bar — describes whatever is selected: a file's
large preview, its kind and format, size, dimensions, who uploaded it and
exactly when, and where it lives; a folder's contents; a summary of several
things; and, with nothing selected, the cluster you're in. The same
[Info](/docs/canvas/cards#info) opens from the board. On the Files page it
opens as a card beside the files (on a phone or tablet, as a sheet from the bottom), and
whether it's open is remembered on your device.
From there:

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Show on board** | Jumps to the card, selected, on the canvas — or **Put on board** for a file that's waiting in Files |
| **Download** | The original file |
| **Copy link** | A deep link that opens the board with the card selected |
| **Delete** | Removes it, with undo |

**Show on board** is the important one: it connects the two views, so finding
something in Files puts you in front of it in context. It and **Copy link**
are app actions, so they are not offered to a signed-out visitor on a shared
link — there is no canvas for them to open, and the deep link would not resolve.

For an audio clip the panel also reports its **length, tempo, key and format**,
which is most of the reason to open it at all.

A file with more than one version lists them under **Versions**, where you can
make an older one current again, download it, label it, or delete it — see
[Versions](/docs/files#versions). Its right-click menu here offers **Upload new
version…** too.

## Adding files here

Drag files into Files, or use **Add files** beside the search — on a phone or
tablet, the **+** in the bottom bar. They join the
cluster without going on its board: they wait in Files, marked
[**Not on board**](#not-on-board), until you drag them onto the board or choose
**Put on board**. The same goes for **Upload** in the sidebar — dropping on the
canvas is how you put files straight on the board. An empty
cluster offers **Add files**, **New cluster** and **Link a cluster** right
where its contents would be.

Drop a single file onto a file of the same kind instead, and it becomes that
file's [next version](/docs/files#versions) — the file lights up and says so
first. Hold `⌥` to add it as a separate file.

## Presence

Files shows who else is in the cluster, and what they have selected — the
same [presence](/docs/collaborate/presence) information the canvas shows, so
switching views does not mean losing sight of your collaborators.

## Every cluster is a drive

This is the framing worth internalising. A cluster is not "a canvas that also
has a list" — it is a set of contents that can be arranged spatially or browsed
like a folder. Teams that come from a shared-drive workflow can use Clusters
that way from day one and discover the canvas later.

That discovery is nudged exactly once, the first time a cluster gets full enough
for it to matter.

## Can I use Clusters like Google Drive?

For the files a project is working from, largely yes — and the difference is
that the same files are also a canvas your team arranges and argues over. For
what a drive is really for, no. Here is the line:

| Like a drive | In Clusters |
|---|---|
| Browse a folder's contents | Yes — the Files view, as a grid or a list |
| Sort and filter | Yes — by name, type, size and dates, filtered to one kind of content |
| Search | Yes — [⌘K](/docs/organize/search) finds files by name, card text and the words inside documents. It does not read text inside images or PDFs |
| Preview without opening | Yes — every item has a real preview, and Quick look shows it full size |
| Download | Yes — one file, or a selection as a zip of up to {{fact:zipMaxFiles}} files or {{fact:zipMaxSize}} |
| Folders inside folders | Yes — nested clusters, as deep as you like |
| Upload a whole folder | Yes — [drop it, or its zip](/docs/clusters#importing-folders-and-zips), and its folders become nested clusters with their files in Files |
| Share | A whole cluster, yes: one link opens it read-only, and everyone you invite edits free |
| Store any kind of file | Yes, at any size, on every plan. The free plan holds {{fact:demoCardLimit}} cards or {{fact:freeStorage}} of files, whichever comes first; {{fact:planName}} removes the card cap and gives you a {{fact:creatorStorage}} drive |
| Keep each file's original name | Yes — images and videos since names were kept; audio, PDFs and attachments always |
| Share one file by its own link | Yes — **Share file…** gives a video, a PDF or any other file a page of its own, without the cluster around it. See [Sharing a single file](/docs/collaborate/sharing#sharing-a-single-file) |
| Sync a folder on your computer | No — there is no desktop sync app |
| Work offline | No |
| Back up your files | No — a cluster is where work happens, not a backup of it |

So use a cluster the way a team uses a project folder: the references, files
and documents a production is working from, organised where everyone on it can
see them. Keep Google Drive, Dropbox or your own server for what a drive does
best — backup, sync to your computer and offline copies.
